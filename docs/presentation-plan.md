# Presentation Prep Plan

Two hours to prepare a 3-minute pitch and a live demo of the voice
navigation assistant. No technical background needed.

## The project in plain words

- **What it is:** an app that guides a blind person through a room using
  only a phone camera and headphones.
- **How it works:** the phone films what is ahead. Our computer works out
  how far away everything is and recognises what things are (chairs,
  people, walls). A voice then says what to do: "Keep going straight",
  "Move a little left", "Stop. Turn right."
- **One-line pitch:** "We turn a phone camera into a voice that walks you
  around obstacles in real time."
- **Why it matters:** a white cane only detects what it touches, within
  about 1 m. Our system sees several metres ahead and gives directions,
  not just warnings.

## The 2 hours

| Time | What he does | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 to 0:20 | Watches the demo: the team walks the route, he watches the screen and listens | He can explain what the camera, depth and labels screens show |
| 0:20 to 0:45 | Learns the 3-minute script below and says it out loud 3 times | He can say it without reading |
| 0:45 to 1:15 | Rehearses the live demo with the team twice, on the real route | Both runs give correct voice guidance |
| 1:15 to 1:35 | Practises the judge questions with a teammate asking them | Each answer takes 2 to 3 sentences |
| 1:35 to 1:50 | One full dress rehearsal with a timer: pitch, demo, questions | Under 5 minutes, no stops |
| 1:50 to 2:00 | Final checklist and a break | Everything ticked |

## 3-minute script

1. **Problem (0:00 to 0:30):** "Millions of blind people move around with
   a cane that only feels what is within reach. Anything further away, like
   a chair or a person walking toward you, they find out about too late."
2. **Solution (0:30 to 1:00):** "We built a voice guide. A phone on your
   chest films the way ahead, and our system tells you in your ear where to
   go: straight, a little left, or stop and turn."
3. **Live demo (1:00 to 2:15):** hand over to the walker. While they walk,
   point at the screen: "This is the camera. This is the depth: closer
   things are brighter. These outlines are what it recognises: people,
   chairs, tables. And you can hear the voice telling them where to go."
4. **What is special (2:15 to 2:45):** "Danger warnings take a fraction of
   a second, because they are decided right here on our computer, not in
   the cloud. It only steers you through gaps wide enough for a person."
5. **Close (2:45 to 3:00):** "A phone, headphones, and a voice that helps
   you walk around obstacles instead of into them. Thank you."

## Live demo run sheet

- **Route:** 8 to 10 m with one or two obstacles, such as a chair or a
  teammate standing in the way, and space to go around them. Practise on
  the exact same route.
- **Roles:** one walker wears the phone and headphones, eyes closed or
  blindfolded if it is safe. A spotter walks beside them for safety. The
  presenter talks and points at the screen.
- **Sound:** play the phone audio through a speaker as well, so the judges
  hear the voice.
- **Backup:** keep a screen recording of a good run ready. If the live demo
  fails, say: "Live demos are brave. Here is one we recorded earlier."

## Likely judge questions

- **"What if the internet drops?"** The obstacle warnings and directions
  work without the internet. They are worked out on our computer.
- **"How fast is it?"** A stop warning starts playing about a fiftieth of a
  second after the camera sees the obstacle.
- **"How is this better than a cane?"** It sees several metres ahead and
  tells you which way to go, not just that something is there.
- **"What is next?"** Running it entirely on the phone, recognising doors
  and stairs, and testing with blind users.
- **"Is it safe?"** It is a hackathon prototype. Real use would need
  testing with blind users and safety checks.
